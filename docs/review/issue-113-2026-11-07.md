The reviews (Lucian Bădescu) give the content of each part. Read together with the plan of the prenote, the series follows §5 (hyperplane sections) number by number: I = nos 1–3 (generic hyperplane section), II = no 4, III = nos 5–8 (exceptional fibres, dimension bound), IV = nos 9–12 (Veronese re-embedding, pencils, Grassmannians), V = no 13 (M. Artin's theorem), VI = no 14 (conic projections), VII = no 15 (axiomatisation). The Addendum proves a lemma that the review says the notes reduce the Bertini statements to without proving it. This part-to-number match comes from the zbMATH titles and reviews; the articles were not read. zbMATH says part VII 1996 is "almost identical" to part VII 1995.

This settles three of the issue's open points:
- **Year:** 1992–1996 (the "1990s" of the MSE snippet is right).
- **Joseph Blass** is a zbMATH author in his own right ("Blass, Joseph", author code `blass.jozef`; in the same first issue he also co-signs a statistics paper with R. A. Padmaraj and V. K. Rohatgi, Ulam Q. 1, No. 1, 1992). I have not looked into how he is related to Piotr Blass and would leave it unstated.
- **Credits differ between sources.** The Circle says the improved §5 is "by P. Blass and Stan Klasa". zbMATH lists Joseph Blass too, on parts I–VI. When citing, take the authors from the journal record. "Stan Klasa" is the form both sources use. Our `editions.json` and `lineage.json` have "Stanisław Klasa", which neither source gives.

## 3. Comparison, and what it tells about dating

**Structure.** In §5 and §6 the translation keeps Grothendieck's numbering: the nos, and the numbers of definitions, propositions, theorems and corollaries. It does not keep the paragraph numbers. "EGA V §1, §2.15–16, §5, §6" are the translators' labels. They are not the plan of the letter of 27.11.1966 (FG_IL_4_EGA5_1_3), where divisors on projective bundles are item 3, ordinary quadratic singularities 4 and hyperplane sections 5. Only §5 agrees with it. The Aiello version itself says its numbering "is of only historical interest". The "former IV n" labels repeat the **typed** numbers of the typescripts: 20 (EGA5_2_1_x), 21 (EGA5_5), 17.15/17.16 (EGA5_3_3). The exception is "former IV 16" for the singular set. It seems to come from the translators' reading of the struck sentence about the text's "natural place", which they render "Par. 16"; the IHÉS transcription reads "par. 19" there. Neither paragraph number is printed in the typescript's own heading.

**Two readings in our IHÉS transcriptions to check on the facsimile:**
- FG_IL_4_EGA5_3_3, heading: we read the struck number as « 27 » ("« 17 » n'est pas exclu"). The translation has "IV.17.15", the text's own cross-references say 17.15.2 and 17.16, and EGA5_3_2 has the marginal « Cf aussi commentaires à IV 17.15 ». **Read « 17 ».**
- FG_IL_4_EGA5_3_2, p. 1, struck end of the 2nd sentence: « par. 19 » (IHÉS transcription) against "Par. 16" (translation). Check the digit.

**Two exemplars.** The translators' copy and the IHÉS copy differ in their hand layer:
- In EGA5_3_2 the IHÉS copy has « 2\add{3} » (a 3 inked over the typed second digit). The translation has "Par. 20" / "V 5 (former IV 20)".
- In §6 the IHÉS copy sends descent to Chap. VI and Brauer–Severi to Chap. VII, where « VII » is overtyped and only the most probable reading. The translation has "chapter V" and "Chapter VI".
- In §5 13.2 both have the hand additions "geometrically irreducible" and "perfect and infinite".

So the source was another annotated copy, as the inventory's "copies de tapuscrit(s) annoté(s)" for 24–25 says. That is one more reason the Montpellier typescript in 25 is worth a look, even though it is "covered" by a translation.

**Dating.**
- The texts: the typed numbering "IV 16/17/20/21" and the plan of État 000 come from a state of the Chap. IV plan earlier than the move of everything after §21 to a new chapter, which the letter of 27.11.1966 proposes. The hand "IV → V" comes after that move. The letter of 29.9.1965 already numbers hyperplane sections 25 and refers to "mon ancien par. 20". If that "ancien par. 20" is the hyperplane text, État 000 predates 29.9.1965, but the letter does not say which text it means. So the typed texts are most likely from 1965–1966. That is earlier than the IHÉS's « 1969 ? » for EGA5_3_2, _3_3 and _5. It is an inference from numbers, not a written date (`FG_IL_4_EGA5_5` already says as much for its own item).
- Folder 25: the translation does not date the folder's copies. It does show that the folder's contents (typescript plus the letter of 29.9.1965) were together when the Blasses translated them, and the translation's queries to "A.G." are consistent with work done in contact with him. The inventory's bracket "[à partir de 1967-1987]" stands. A proposed reading for `proposed-datings.json`: typescript c. 1965–1966, copies assembled by 1987. Hold it until folder 25 has been opened and its page headings set against the Aiello table of contents.
- Folder 24: the translation is 1987 at the earliest, by the inventory's footnote, and the published parts are 1992–1996. If folder 24 is a state of it, its upper bound of 1987 may be too early. To check on the folder itself.
