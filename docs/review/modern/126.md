# Review of the modernised reading 126 (working draft)

Reading reviewed: `transcripts/126/126.modern.tex` (pass of 2026-10-03, revised 2026-10-18 and 2026-11-24), against `transcripts/126/batch-01.fr.tex` (pp. 1-20) and `transcripts/126/batch-02.fr.tex` (pp. 21-34). The facsimiles were not consulted: "the page" below means the transcription. Simulated referee report requested by the author. It was processed locally; only bibliographic lookups went out (Numdam, Crossref, arXiv, Stacks, the SGA 3 re-edition). This is a working draft for the author to check, not a verdict.

## Summary verdict

The reading is careful and mostly sound. The fpqc Čech material (pp. 1-3), the deformation of flat equivalence relations (pp. 4-7), the generated topologies (pp. 9-14) and the non-finite descent example (pp. 31-34) are correct as restated. Most of their footnoted departures are real and correctly described: the sheaf on S rather than X, the |G| components of T ×_S T, the added trivial decomposition group, the exchanged t'' and t''' on p. 28, and the reading of Lemma 2 as "sur Y'".

There are three substantive problems:

- In the generic-descent section (pp. 26-29), one condition defining W' makes the asserted surjectivity false.
- In the same section, the agreement of φ' with φ is claimed on a larger open set than the argument reaches.
- In the deformation Proposition, a hypothesis is presented as automatic.

All three can be fixed locally. The minor issues are mostly about fidelity: a few silent departures, quotations that expand his abbreviations or drop illegible words without […], and one internal inconsistency in the Lean footnotes on pp. 17-23.

Counts: 3 major, 17 minor.

## Major issues

### M1. W' cannot surject onto W ×_{T×T} W with the fifth condition

- **Location**: section « Descente générique », the paragraph « ψ descend » (the definition of W' ⊂ T^5), and its footnote on the order of the triplets (pp. 27-28).
- **Observation**: W' is defined by five conditions in V. The fifth is (t_1, t''', t_2) ∈ V. Since V ⊂ ∩ p_ij^{-1}(U), this forces (t_1, t_2) ∈ U. But a point (t_1, t', t'', t_2) of W ×_{T×T} W has an arbitrary pair (t_1, t_2), because π : W → T ×_S T is surjective. So the points with (t_1, t_2) ∉ U are not in the image of W', and the claim "W' → W ×_{T×T} W est plat et surjectif" is false as W' is defined.
- **Evidence**: the computation of the reading (lines 903-911) uses only the first four triplets. The footnote says the last triplet "sert à la comparaison finale de φ et φ'". It does not: the final comparison (p. 29) takes t' = t_2 and never uses W'. The page's list (batch-02, p. 27) also ends with (t''', t_1, t_2), added as a subscript, and the page itself hedges the surjectivity with « sauf erreur ».
- **Why it matters**: the descent of ψ, and so the existence of φ', rests on W' → W ×_{T×T} W being a morphism of F-descent. With the fifth condition the step fails.
- **Proposed fix**: define W' by the four conditions (t_1, t''', t'), (t''', t', t_2), (t_1, t''', t''), (t''', t'', t_2) ∈ V. Then each condition is dense in the fibre in the variable t''', by the reductions: the relevant pairs (t_1, t'), (t', t_2), (t_1, t''), (t'', t_2) lie in the shrunken U. Say in a footnote that the page's fifth triplet is dropped because it would force (t_1, t_2) ∈ U. Delete the claim that it serves the final comparison.

### M2. Agreement of φ' with φ is claimed on the original U but proved only on the shrunken one

- **Location**: the Proposition of « Descente générique » (« telle que φ' = φ au-dessus de U ∩ (T' ×_S T') »), the footnote to « Réductions » (« tout en gardant φ sur l'ouvert U d'origine »), and the paragraph « φ' prolonge φ ».
- **Observation**: the reading keeps φ on the original U and builds W from the shrunken U (U_1 ∩ T'^2). It then argues: for (t_1, t_2, t_3) ∈ V take t' = t_2, and conclude because « p_13 : V → U est plat et surjectif ». This has two gaps.
  - (t_1, t_2, t_3) is in W only if (t_1, t_2) and (t_2, t_3) lie in U_1. Triplets of V give pairs in the original U only.
  - The surjectivity of p_13 : V → U is not given for the original U, and is not argued. V is only a dense open with V ⊂ ∩ p_ij^{-1}(U).

  So equality is established at most over U_1 ∩ (T' × T'). In a general fibred category, agreement on a dense open does not extend to the original U.
- **Evidence**: batch-02 p. 26 replaces U by U_1 (« Remplaçant 𝒰 par 𝒰_1 »). On the page, p. 29's « V → 𝒰 est surjectif » therefore concerns the shrunken set. The overstatement comes from the reading's own decision to keep the original U.
- **Why it matters**: the conclusion of the Proposition, as stated, is not proved and may fail.
- **Proposed fix**: state the agreement over U_1 ∩ (T' ×_S T'). In the last step replace V by V_1 = V ∩ p_12^{-1}(U_1) ∩ p_23^{-1}(U_1) ∩ T'^3. For τ = (t_1, t_3) ∈ U_1 ∩ T'^2, the fibre of p_13 meets V_1. The reason is that V and the two U_1-conditions are each dense in that fibre, the T'-condition is a nonempty open, and dense opens meet. So p_13 : V_1 → U_1 ∩ T'^2 is flat and surjective, and t' = t_2 is allowed on V_1. Keeping φ on the original U is still needed for the pairs extracted from the triplets in W' and W''', so the footnote can stay, but the Proposition must not claim more than U_1.

### M3. R ×_X X_0 "by either projection" is a hypothesis, not a fact

- **Location**: the Proposition of « Déformation d'une relation d'équivalence plate », « posons R_0 = R ×_X X_0 (par l'une ou l'autre projection), de sorte que R_0 ⇉ X_0 est une relation d'équivalence ».
- **Observation**: p_1^{-1}(X_0) and p_2^{-1}(X_0) are in general different closed subschemes of R, even when both p_i are flat. Counterexample: let X be the disjoint union of two copies of Spec k[x]/(x²) and R the equivalence relation identifying the two copies (R = X ⊔ X, with the identity and the swap). Take X_0 to reduce only the second copy. Then p_1^{-1}(X_0) ≠ p_2^{-1}(X_0), and neither of them defines an equivalence relation on X_0. The hypothesis needed is that X_0 is R-stable: p_1^{-1}(X_0) = p_2^{-1}(X_0), i.e. p_1^*𝒥_X·𝒪_R = p_2^*𝒥_X·𝒪_R.
- **Evidence**: the footnote already says this equality is unread on the page but necessary. The parenthesis in the statement still presents it as automatic.
- **Why it matters**: the statement is the reading's own. As written it is not well posed. The later steps need the equality: the two isomorphisms p_i^*𝒥_X ≅ 𝒥_R and the final isomorphism R_0 ≅ X_0 ×_{Y_0} X_0.
- **Proposed fix**: write « supposons p_1^{-1}(X_0) = p_2^{-1}(X_0) =: R_0 (X_0 est stable par R) ; alors R_0 ⇉ X_0 est une relation d'équivalence ». Keep the footnote's explanation of why this is needed.

8. **p. 17, the remark on « d constant ».** The footnote says that « telle quelle, l'assertion ne tient pas ».
