\section{Calibration: what the generator re-derives}\label{sec:calibration}

A theorem generator should be tested on inputs whose outputs are known. Run on the simplest decks, ours produces the following results, all in print except one, which Grothendieck states as an exercise in an unpublished note; three of them (the finite-monoid criterion, essential points as idempotents, the subtopoi of $\mathbb N$-sets) we first believed new, before the literature search of stage~6 found them. For the other rows the article does not record whether we believed them new. We list them only to show that the machine is sound; they are not claimed as new. One output deserves a word, because its reading is new even though the mathematics is known. For an abstract rewriting system $(S,\to)$, give $S$ the topology whose open sets are the sets of terms closed under rewriting. Then \emph{the system is confluent (Church--Rosser) iff this space is extremally disconnected}: this is the third line of the table applied to the preorder $\to^*$. Confluence of $\beta$-reduction thus says that the space of $\lambda$-terms, with the sets closed under $\beta$-reduction as open sets, is extremally disconnected. We did not find this reading in the rewriting literature, but since it is a direct restatement of a published theorem we do not count it as new.
