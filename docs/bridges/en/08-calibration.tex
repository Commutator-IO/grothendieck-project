\section{Calibration: what the generator re-derives}\label{sec:calibration}

A theorem generator should be tested on inputs whose outputs are known. On the simplest decks, ours produces the results below. All are in print except one, which Grothendieck states as an exercise in an unpublished note. We believed three of them new (the finite-monoid criterion, essential points as idempotents, the subtopoi of $\mathbb N$-sets) until the literature search of stage~6 found them; for the other rows we did not record whether we believed them new. We list them only to show that the machine is sound, and we claim none of them as new. For the run itself, we recorded only the following counts.

The confluence line has a reading that is new even though its mathematics is known. For an abstract rewriting system $(S,\to)$, give $S$ the topology whose open sets are the sets of terms closed under rewriting. Then \emph{the system is confluent (Church--Rosser) iff this space is extremally disconnected}: this is the third line of the table applied to the preorder $\to^*$. Confluence of $\beta$-reduction thus says that the space of $\lambda$-terms, with the sets closed under $\beta$-reduction as open sets, is extremally disconnected. We did not find this reading in the rewriting literature, but since it is a direct restatement of a published theorem we do not count it as new.
