# Comments to the author

## Summary

The paper computes Pic[M_{0,n}/𝔖_{n−1}] ≅ ℤ/(n−1)(n−2), generated by the tangent line L_n at the fixed point, and the order n−1 of L = ⊗L_i in Pic[M_{0,n}/𝔖_n], in characteristic zero. The method is to put x_n = ∞, which turns the stack into monic squarefree polynomials of degree m = n−1 modulo the affine group, then to centre the roots, which leaves [V_m/𝔾_m] with weights 2, …, m and the discriminant of weight m(m−1). An elementary section gives the same orders in a model of vectors in k², with explicit trivialising functions and fixed configurations, and that part is formalised in Lean. The motivation is a table on page 70 of Grothendieck's folder 146.

## Strengths

- The main computation is right, short and complete in its essentials. I re-derived each step (below) and found no error in the mathematics of Section 2.
- The two proofs fit together: the stack proof gives the group, the vector model gives explicit trivialising functions and the lower bounds. Their conventions agree (checked below).
- The literature is stated with care: the even case is credited to Edidin–Hu, and the paper does not claim priority beyond the odd case.
- The paper is honest about what Lean covers: the dictionary between equivariant line bundles and functions is stated as not formalised.

## Step-by-step check

1. **Slice.** 𝔖_{n−1} × PGL₂ acts on Conf_n(ℙ¹); the locus x_n = ∞ is a slice for PGL₂ with stabiliser Aff (PGL₂ is transitive on ℙ¹). So [M/𝔖_{n−1}] ≅ [Conf_m(𝔸¹)/(Aff × 𝔖_m)]. 𝔖_m acts freely on ordered distinct m-tuples with quotient U_m. Hence [U_m/Aff]. No hypothesis on k. Correct.
2. **Centring.** 𝔾_a is normal in Aff. Translation by b changes c_1 by −mb, so when m is invertible each 𝔾_a-orbit meets c_1 = 0 exactly once and U_m ≅ 𝔾_a × V_m, compatibly with 𝔾_m (scaling preserves c_1 = 0). Hence [U_m/Aff] ≅ [V_m/𝔾_m], with c_j of weight j. Correct; it uses m ∈ k^×, which characteristic zero gives.
3. **Discriminant.** As a function of the roots, ∏_{i<j}(r_i − r_j)², of weight m(m−1). Correct. Irreducibility is asserted without a reason (m1).
4. **Lemma 2.** The result ℤ/m(m−1), generated by the identity character, is correct. The proof uses an equivariant localisation sequence that is neither cited nor stated (m2). A proof by units is shorter and self-contained: Pic V_m = 0, a linearisation of 𝒪 is a unit of 𝒪(V_m)[t^{±1}] satisfying the cocycle rule, these units are c·disc^j·t^a, so every linearisation is a character; two characters give isomorphic bundles iff their difference is the weight of a unit, and the units have weights in m(m−1)ℤ.
5. **Weights.** In u = 1/x, x ↦ ax + b is u ↦ u/(a + bu), derivative a^{−1} at u = 0: weight −1 on T_∞. At a finite root the derivative is a: weight +1. The tensor product of the m lines T_{r_i}, each trivialised by ∂/∂x, carries no sign under permutation of the factors. So L pulls back to −1 + m = n − 2, whose order in ℤ/(n−1)(n−2) is n − 1. Correct.
6. **Vector model.** T_{⟨v⟩}ℙ¹ ≅ ⟨v⟩^{⊗−2} ⊗ det k². A section of L^{⊗e} is a function F homogeneous of degree 2e in each v_i, with F(gv) = det(g)^{ne} F(v). Check on scalars: F(tv) = t^{2ne}F(v) = det(tI)^{ne}F(v), consistent with the trivial action of the centre. For L_n^{⊗e}: degree 2e in v_n, 0 elsewhere, det(g)^e; scalars again consistent. Correct.
7. **Upper bounds.** ∏_{i≠j}[v_iv_j]: degree 2(n−1) in each v_i, n(n−1) brackets, so det^{n(n−1)}, invariant under 𝔖_n, and equal to (−1)^{n(n−1)/2}∏_{i<j}[v_iv_j]². For L_n: numerator degree 2(n−1)(n−2) in v_n; each other v_i has degree 2(n−2) in the numerator and 2(n−2) in the denominator; det weight 2(n−1)(n−2) − (n−1)(n−2) = (n−1)(n−2). Correct.
8. **Lower bounds.** diag(ω, 1) fixes (0, 1) and permutes the vectors (z, 1), z ∈ μ_{n−1}, as vectors; so F(gv) = F(v) by symmetry and = ω^e F(v) by equivariance. diag(ζ, 1) with {0, ∞} ∪ μ_{n−2} multiplies (1, 0) by ζ, which F of degree 0 in that vector does not see. Coprimality gives (n−1)(n−2) | e. Correct; it needs the roots of unity, which over a general field of characteristic zero means passing to k̄ (m5).
9. **Consistency between the two proofs.** Section 2 gives order m(m−1) = (n−1)(n−2) for L_n and n−1 for L; Section 3 gives the same, with the same sign-free statements. The fibre action of diag(ω, 1) at 0 is ω, which matches the derivative of z ↦ ωz at 0 (weight +1 at a finite point, as in step 5).
10. **Remark (n = 3).** [M_{0,3}/𝔖_2] = B(ℤ/2), Pic = ℤ/2 = ℤ/(2·1). Correct.

## Major comments

### M1. The account of page 72 contradicts the theorem without saying so

- Location: § 1, « The source », sentence on page 72.
- Observation: the paper says page 72 « concludes that L^{⊗6} is trivial for n = 4 and n = 5 ». For n = 5 the theorem says L has order 4, so L^{⊗6} ≅ L^{⊗2} is not trivial. The paper then says the theorem « fills in the table », without noting that the n = 5 statement of page 72, as transcribed, is false.
- Evidence: `transcripts/146/batch-04.fr.tex`, p. 72: « Ex n = 5, ν = 6, \uncertain{on trouve} L^{⊗6} ≃ 1 », with « on trouve » an uncertain reading; p. 70 below the table: a computation ending in (L⁴)³ ≃ 𝒪, the 𝒪 struck. The findings entry records the same disagreement with the « 12 ? » of the table. The first row of the table, (n−1)(n−2) = 6, 12, 20, is also on the page and is not mentioned.
- Why it matters: a reader comparing with the facsimile will find the paper silently endorsing a false statement, or will think the theorem is wrong.
- Requested action: report page 72 as read (n = 4: L^{⊗6} ≅ 1, true and weaker than order 3; n = 5: L^{⊗6} ≅ 1, read with an uncertain « on trouve », which the theorem contradicts), mention the (L⁴)³ computation of page 70, and the row (n−1)(n−2) of the table.

### M2. The letter m has two meanings

- Location: § 1 (« the least m ≥ 1 »), § 2 (m = n − 1), § 3 and Figure 1 (exponent m).
- Observation: in Section 3, « transforming by det(g)^m » and « (n−1)(n−2) divides m » use m for the exponent, while Section 2 fixed m = n − 1.
- Requested action: call the exponent e throughout §§ 1 and 3 and in the caption.

### M3. The attribution of the Picard group of [M_{0,n}/𝔖_n] is imprecise

- Location: § 1, « What was known », first sentence; Remark 4.
- Observation: Arsie–Vistoli compute the Picard groups of stacks of smooth uniform cyclic covers (Th. 5.1: cyclic of order r(rd−1)n·gcd(d, n+1)) and deduce Pic H_g (Remark 5.5); they do not state Pic[M_{0,n}/𝔖_n]. Gorchinskiy–Viviani's Th. 3.6 gives Pic D_{2g+2} = ℤ/(4g+2) for g ≥ 2 and char k ∤ 2g+2, where D_{2g+2} = [B_sm(2, 2g+2)/PGL₂] (Prop. 3.4), that is [M_{0,n}/𝔖_n] for n = 2g+2 ≥ 6, not for all even n. The Remark says the same computation « recovers the results » of both papers, without showing it.
- Requested action: say that GV state the group for even n ≥ 6, that AV's method (binary forms, discriminant) computes it, and in the Remark give the computation: with H = (GL₂ × 𝔾_m)/𝔾_m acting on smooth forms, the characters are the pairs (a, b) with 2a + nb = 0, and the discriminant gives ℤ/2(n−1) for n even and ℤ/(n−1) for n odd. I re-did this computation; it agrees with GV for n even and with AV's formula read at r = 1, d = n (an inference, not a statement of AV).

## Minor comments

- **m1** (§ 2, discriminant). Irreducibility of the discriminant of the centred polynomial is asserted. Requested: one line. The generic discriminant is irreducible in k[c_1, …, c_m] and invariant under translation; since m is invertible, k[c_1, …, c_m] = k[c_1, c'_2, …, c'_m] with c' the coefficients of the centred polynomial, and the discriminant lies in k[c'_2, …, c'_m], where it stays irreducible.
- **m2** (Lemma 2, proof). The exact sequence is the localisation sequence of equivariant Chow groups (Edidin–Graham) together with Pic = A¹ for smooth quotient stacks; neither is cited. Requested: replace with the units argument of step 4, which needs no reference. The sign in « the class of the divisor of a semi-invariant of weight w is the character of weight w » depends on conventions; only the subgroup matters.
- **m4** (§ 2, proof of (2)). « Amounts to giving a function » is the descent along the 𝔾_m^n-torsor of vectors over configurations, and the passage from PGL₂ to GL₂ is allowed because the centre acts trivially on ⟨v⟩^{−2} ⊗ det. Requested: half a sentence.
- **m5** (§ 3, lower bound). The roots of unity need not be in k. Requested: say that one may extend scalars to an algebraic closure, since a trivialisation over k gives one over k̄, and the upper-bound functions are defined over k.
- **m6** (§ 1, n = 4). « Pic ℳ_{1,1} = ℤ/12 gives the order 6 » skips the step. Requested: pull-back along the μ₂-gerbe is injective with image the classes of μ₂-weight 0, which is 2ℤ/12; L_4 pulls back to T_0E^{⊗2} = λ^{−2}, with λ the Hodge bundle, a generator of ℤ/12.
- **m7** (§ 1, the source). Folder 146 is titled « Groupoïdes de Teichmüller »; « on the action of symmetric groups on M_{0,n} » describes these pages, not the folder.
- **m8** (§ 1). Give the MathOverflow URL.
- **m9** (§ 3, Lean). The Lean statements take F an arbitrary function on K-points, which makes the lower bound stronger than stated and the upper bound the same; worth one clause. Their hypotheses (n ≥ 2, resp. n ≥ 3, primitive roots of unity in K) match the text.
- **m10** (characteristic). The proof uses characteristic zero only for m ∈ k^×, the irreducibility and reducedness of the discriminant, and the roots of unity. Positive characteristic was not examined in this review; no change requested, the theorem is stated in characteristic zero.
