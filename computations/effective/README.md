# Effectivity of finite equivalence relations (problems paper, Problem 14)

Grothendieck's question (FGA, exposé 212, §2; Buffalo, 11 July 1973): for X finite over a field k and R ⊂ X ×_k X a closed equivalence relation, finite but not flat over X, is R → X ×_Y X an isomorphism, Y = Spec Γ(X, O_X)^R?

Algebraically: A = Γ(X, O_X), I ⊂ A ⊗ A the ideal of R. The conditions on I are:

- reflexive: I ⊆ ker μ;
- symmetric: σ(I) = I;
- transitive: p₁₃(I) ⊆ I ⊗ A + A ⊗ I.

B = {a : a⊗1 − 1⊗a ∈ I}. R is effective iff I equals J_B, the ideal generated by the b⊗1 − 1⊗b.

`search.py` takes random ideals I₀ ⊆ ker μ containing J_{B₀}, for random subalgebras B₀. It computes the largest equivalence relation E ⊆ I₀ by iterating I ← I ∩ σ(I) ∩ p₁₃⁻¹(I⊗A + A⊗I), and tests E = J_{A^E}. It runs over F_p, on monomial algebras k[x₁..x_v]/(order ideal).

```bash
python3 search.py 2 400 3 all 6   # all 127 monomial algebras of dimension ≤ 6 in 1–3 variables, over F_2
```

Results are in `out-p2.txt` and `out-p3.txt`. A counterexample found here would be checked in Lean.

## Result (2026-11-07)

No counterexample. Over F_2 (400 trials per algebra) and F_3 (300), on the 48 monomial algebras of dimension 2 to 6 in 1 to 3 variables (all variables used), every equivalence relation reached was effective. This is a probe of small cases, not evidence for the general statement: the random relations are biased toward large ones, and the closure often collapses to a relation generated by its invariants.

## Answer (2026-11-07): a non-effective relation on a finite scheme

The random search was looking in the wrong place. Raicu's non-effective finite equivalence relation on 𝔸² (appendix to Kollár, *Quotients by finite equivalence relations*, ¶ 50; Raicu, Proc. AMS 138 (2010), Example 2.1), restricted to the R-stable finite subscheme Z = V(f₁³, f₂³, f₃²) of length 27 (f₁ = x₁², f₂ = x₁x₂ − x₂², f₃ = x₂³), is a closed equivalence relation on a finite k-scheme which is not effective, over every field. The reduction is the edition's and has not been checked by a specialist.

- `raicu/check.py P`: Raicu's relation on 𝔸², the equivalence-relation axioms degree by degree and the degree-5 gap (P = 2, 3, 5, or 0 for ℚ).
- `raicu/full.py P`: on the 27-dimensional algebra of Z, all invariants of degree ≤ 7, and the dimensions of I_Z and J_B + K in degrees 1 to 14. They differ in degrees 5 and 6 only (over 𝔽₃: 44 against 43, and 77 against 75).
- `raicu/hilb.py`: the Hilbert function of Z (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 4, 2).

Monomial algebras are close to monoid algebras, where Raicu's Theorem 3.1 makes the Amitsur complex exact, which may be why the search above found nothing.

## Every characteristic (7 November 2026)

`raicu/certify.py` reruns `raicu/full.py` over Q and records the prime factors of every pivot of the
eliminations: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 41, 61, 67, 661. For any other prime the same elimination runs over
F_p with the same pivots, so the ranks and the gap (44 > 43 in degree 5, 77 > 75 in degree 6) are
those over Q. Each listed prime was checked directly with `full.py`, as were all primes up to 101,
1009 and 10007 (`raicu/out-primes.txt`). The witness (x1y2 − x2y1)y2³ lies in (I+K)₅ and not in
(J+K)₅ over Q, F2, F3 and F5. Written up in `docs/effective/article.tex`.
